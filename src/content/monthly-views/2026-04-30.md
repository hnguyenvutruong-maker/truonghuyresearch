## Executive Summary
April 2026 should be read as constructive on price repair but incomplete on ownership confirmation. The VN-Index opened at 1,661.53, traded as high as 1,888.99, fell as low as 1,634.55, and closed at 1,854.10. That produced a +11.59% month, so the headline result was a gain; but the real story is the interaction between the monthly candle, the liquidity base, the foreign-flow line, and the sector map. Average daily liquidity reached VND 21,303bn across 24 sessions, which means the move cannot be dismissed as a thin or purely technical print. There was enough value traded for the market to reveal real positioning preferences.

The month was shaped by an oversold post-March starting point followed by a sentiment reset from market-upgrade roadmap news. Foreign investors recorded net selling of VND 13,741.46bn, with VND 55,854.59bn of buy value against VND 69,596.04bn of sell value where data is available. That ownership signal is crucial. A market can rise with foreign selling if domestic liquidity is deep, but that kind of rally is more dependent on margin appetite, broker balance sheets, and the willingness of local institutions to absorb supply. A market that falls with foreign selling has a cleaner defensive message because local and offshore investors are aligned in reducing risk.

Sector rotation also mattered. Technology was the best sector at +14.20%, while Oil & Gas was the worst sector at -6.80%, leaving a leader-laggard spread of 21.00 percentage points. That spread says the market was not moving as one block. Investors were making clear choices about which earnings streams, balance sheets, and macro exposures were worth owning. The practical portfolio read is therefore not simply whether the VN-Index was up or down. It is whether the index move had sponsorship, breadth, and macro alignment. For April 2026, the answer is mixed: the tape offered tradable signals, but the quality of those signals depended heavily on foreign flow and sector leadership.

## Market Structure and Tape Quality
The first structural point is the relationship between the open, the close, and the monthly range. The index closed at 1,854.10, above the opening of 1,661.53 — a +11.59% gain across the month. The high at 1,888.99 was approached but not sustained, while the low at 1,634.55 was set early and never retested. A close near the top of the range after an opening near the low is an accumulation structure: buyers controlled direction throughout, even if they paused short of the high.

The second point is liquidity. Average daily turnover of VND 21,303bn across 24 sessions was the lowest of the five-month sample, which modestly qualifies the size of the rally. The +11.59% gain on lower-than-average liquidity suggests the move was partly driven by a reduced sell-side rather than aggressive buy-side conviction — consistent with a relief rally rather than a fresh institutional accumulation cycle.

The third point is ownership quality. Foreign net selling of VND 13,741.46bn against a +11.59% index gain is the defining tension of April: price recovered sharply while offshore accounts continued to reduce. Domestic investors absorbed that supply and drove the index higher, which confirms that local re-risking — not offshore reversal — was the primary force.

The fourth point is sector breadth. The 21.00-percentage-point spread between Technology (+14.20%) and Oil & Gas (-6.80%) confirms a directional rotation: the March oil hedge was unwound and growth names were re-accumulated by domestic accounts.

## Macro Regime
The macro regime for April 2026 was defined by the interaction between DXY, USD/VND, WTI, gold, and BTC. DXY closed at 98.92 with a -1.04% monthly move. USD/VND closed at 26,355 with a +0.12% monthly move. Those two numbers are the core FX channel. DXY fell while USD/VND was nearly unchanged — a marginal improvement in the EM-allocation backdrop that removed March's dollar-pressure headwind without fully restoring the January FX tailwind. The domestic currency matters because it shapes SBV liquidity posture, foreign investor confidence, import-cost expectations, and the willingness of local investors to use leverage.

That FX improvement let investors refocus on reform, liquidity, and earnings even though oil stayed elevated. This point should not be treated as a generic macro overlay. For Vietnam, FX stability is part of the equity risk premium. If USD/VND is calm, investors can focus on earnings revisions, sector rotation, and domestic liquidity. If USD/VND becomes unstable, the market starts pricing the risk of policy tightening, dollar hoarding, and weaker foreign participation. That is why a small monthly move in USD/VND can still have a large psychological effect when it happens near a stress zone.

WTI closed at USD 106.88 with a +5.43% move, remaining above USD 100 after March's spike. Oil stayed elevated enough to sustain Oil & Gas sector pressure on the broader cost base, but the rate of change slowed sharply compared to March's +44.43% shock. The equity market treated that deceleration as partial relief even though the absolute level remained high — Oil & Gas reversed to worst sector at -6.80% as capital rotated away from the commodity hedge.

Gold and BTC added a second layer of risk-sentiment information. Gold closed at USD 4,545.20 with a -2.20% monthly move, while BTC closed at USD 75,776.13 with a +11.05% monthly move. The cross-asset message was constructive: BTC strength (+11.05%) alongside a mild gold decline (-2.20%) signals a return of speculative liquidity and risk appetite, consistent with the domestic equity recovery. This is the inverse of February's defensive gold surge and the March cross-asset collapse.

## VN-Index Monthly Review
The VN-Index path matters because the same monthly return can come from very different investor behavior. In April 2026, the index opened at 1,661.53, reached a high of 1,888.99, printed a low of 1,634.55, and closed at 1,854.10. The support level for the next month is 1,634.55; the resistance level is 1,888.99. Those are not arbitrary chart points. They are the places where investors either proved willing to defend risk or refused to pay higher prices.

The opening level sets the benchmark for the month. Closing at 1,854.10 against an open of 1,661.53 confirms buyers controlled the outcome. The low at 1,634.55 — below even the March close of 1,674.49 — was set early, making the V-shape recovery the defining price structure of the month. The high at 1,888.99 was not sustained, leaving a ceiling that April's buyers could not clear. Together, these four numbers describe a sharp repair rally led by domestic re-risking rather than confirmed offshore accumulation — the close is strong but the uncleared high is the unresolved question heading into May.

Liquidity completes the review. VND 21,303bn of average daily value across 24 sessions shows that this was an investable tape. There was enough turnover for institutional and active domestic accounts to reposition. That matters because technical levels are more reliable when they are formed on real liquidity. A support level formed on thin holiday trading is weaker. A support level formed after heavy value traded is more meaningful because it reflects actual balance-sheet commitment.

## Foreign Flow and Ownership Quality
Foreign investors bought VND 55,854.59bn and sold VND 69,596.04bn, resulting in net selling of VND 13,741.46bn. The figures are based on HOSE foreign-trading data available for the month, keeping the flow discussion anchored to reported buy and sell values rather than proxy values.

For April 2026, net selling of VND 13,741.46bn — VND 55,854.59bn bought against VND 69,596.04bn sold — means the +11.59% rally was entirely domestically funded. The foreign outflow was smaller than March's VND 17,607.83bn, which is a relative improvement, but it remained large in absolute terms. The combination of a +11.59% price gain with VND 13,741bn of foreign selling is the clearest example in the series of domestic-absorption driving a major index move. That configuration is sustainable as long as domestic liquidity and margin capacity hold, but it creates asymmetric vulnerability: any deterioration in local risk appetite faces no offshore support floor.

## Sector Rotation Map
Technology at +14.20% leading after its -15.20% March collapse is the defining rotation of April: domestic accounts re-accumulated quality growth at the discounted price produced by March's forced selling. Oil & Gas at -6.80% simultaneously unwound March's commodity hedge — investors rotated out of the oil-shock winner and back into beta. That pair of moves is a coherent domestic portfolio rebalancing, not a random sector scatter. The 21.00-percentage-point spread confirms the conviction behind the rotation: the same investors who may have sold Technology in March and bought Oil & Gas were reversing course in April.

## Global Cross-Asset Synthesis
The global cross-asset picture should be translated into Vietnam-specific channels. DXY closed at 98.92, USD/VND at 26,355, gold at USD 4,545.20, WTI at USD 106.88, and BTC at USD 75,776.13. These are not just dashboard numbers. Each one maps into a different part of the Vietnam equity risk premium.

Read as one synthesis rather than asset by asset, April's cross-asset set turned from the broad March headwind into a partial tailwind: the FX channel eased, the oil move decelerated into a known input rather than a fresh shock, and a returning risk-appetite signal replaced the prior two months' defensiveness. That combination supported the domestic re-risking that drove the index recovery.

## Risk Scenarios and Technical Levels
The technical framework for May starts with support at 1,634.55 and resistance at 1,888.99. A close through either level should be treated as signal, not noise. Support is where buyers previously defended the market. Resistance is where marginal demand previously failed. If the index closes below support, investors should assume the prior range has broken. If the index closes above resistance, investors should assume underweight accounts may need to chase.

The bull case requires three things. First, foreign selling must slow or turn into buying. Second, liquidity must remain high enough to absorb supply without forcing price concessions. Third, leadership must broaden beyond Technology so the index is not dependent on one sector. If those conditions are met, the market can extend toward a higher range and convert the current setup into a more durable trend.

The base case is range trading. In that scenario, the VN-Index holds above 1,634.55 but fails to clear 1,888.99. Sector rotation remains active, domestic liquidity remains the main support, and foreign flow is either mixed or modestly negative. This is a stock-picking regime. It rewards sector selection and punishes passive assumptions. It also requires risk controls because breakouts and breakdowns can fail quickly when ownership quality is not decisive.

The bear case is a support break with renewed foreign outflow. That would imply the market is losing domestic absorption capacity at the same time offshore accounts are reducing risk. The trigger could be FX pressure, a WTI shock, disappointing macro data, weak earnings revisions, or a policy headline. In that case, the first response should be to reduce cyclical beta, avoid crowded winners that are losing momentum, and wait for either foreign-flow stabilization or a reclaim of the broken support.

## The Month Ahead
The main risk was that price had repaired faster than foreign flow, making the rally sensitive to any renewed macro shock. The practical stance for May is therefore conditional rather than dogmatic. The index level matters, but the quality of the move matters more. A breakout with weak breadth and foreign selling is less compelling than a smaller advance with improving ownership and broader sector participation. A pullback with stable foreign flow is less dangerous than a pullback with accelerating offshore supply.

The first data point to watch is foreign net flow. The second is USD/VND. The third is WTI. The fourth is whether Technology continues to lead or whether leadership broadens into banks, securities, industrials, and consumption. Those indicators together will say whether the market is entering a healthier accumulation phase or simply rotating inside a fragile range. They should be tracked weekly, not only at month-end.

For portfolio construction, the right approach is to respect the range and avoid over-interpreting a single headline index close. Above 1,888.99, upside momentum deserves more respect. Below 1,634.55, capital preservation becomes more important than buying dips. Inside the range, sector selection matters most. Investors should prefer sectors with improving earnings visibility, clean ownership support, and macro alignment, while avoiding sectors where price strength is purely a function of positioning squeeze.

The final takeaway is straightforward: monthly notes cannot be short because the market is not one-dimensional. Price, liquidity, foreign ownership, sector breadth, FX, oil, gold, and global risk appetite all interact. A useful Monthly Market View has to connect those pieces, explain where confidence is high, state where data coverage is incomplete, and define the levels that would prove the thesis wrong. That is the standard this note applies to April 2026.

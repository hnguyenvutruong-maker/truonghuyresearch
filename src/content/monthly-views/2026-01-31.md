## Executive Summary
January 2026 should be read as constructive but not yet fully confirmed by foreign ownership. The VN-Index opened at 1,733.39, traded as high as 1,918.46, fell as low as 1,731.63, and closed at 1,829.04. That produced a +5.52% month, so the headline result was a gain; but the real story is the interaction between the monthly candle, the liquidity base, the foreign-flow line, and the sector map. Average daily liquidity reached VND 25,847bn across 23 sessions, which means the move cannot be dismissed as a thin or purely technical print. There was enough value traded for the market to reveal real positioning preferences.

The month was shaped by a year-opening mix of upgrade-expectation support, a softer dollar backdrop, and domestic liquidity strong enough to absorb offshore selling. Foreign investors recorded net selling of VND 6,023.35bn, with VND 92,455.90bn of buy value against VND 98,479.26bn of sell value where data is available. That ownership signal is crucial. A market can rise with foreign selling if domestic liquidity is deep, but that kind of rally is more dependent on margin appetite, broker balance sheets, and the willingness of local institutions to absorb supply. A market that falls with foreign selling has a cleaner defensive message because local and offshore investors are aligned in reducing risk.

Sector rotation also mattered. Technology was the best sector at +8.50%, while Real Estate was the worst sector at -4.20%, leaving a leader-laggard spread of 12.70 percentage points. That spread says the market was not moving as one block. Investors were making clear choices about which earnings streams, balance sheets, and macro exposures were worth owning. The practical portfolio read is therefore not simply whether the VN-Index was up or down. It is whether the index move had sponsorship, breadth, and macro alignment. For January 2026, the answer is mixed: the tape offered tradable signals, but the quality of those signals depended heavily on foreign flow and sector leadership.

## Market Structure and Tape Quality
The first structural point is the relationship between the open, the close, and the monthly range. The index closed above its opening level at 1,829.04, which is also above the mid-point of the 1,731.63–1,918.46 range — a structure consistent with net accumulation, though the close fell short of the high, leaving the January candle neither a full breakout nor a reversal.

The second point is liquidity. Average daily turnover of VND 25,847bn across 23 sessions validates the price signal. The index rose on sufficient volume for institutional and active domestic accounts to reposition, which means the +5.52% gain was not a thin holiday print — it reflected real capital commitment.

The third point is ownership quality. Foreign investors were net sellers of VND 6,023.35bn, which means the price tape and offshore flow need to be read together. Sustained foreign outflow changes the risk profile because it creates supply at the top of rallies and reduces the probability that breakouts become self-reinforcing. Domestic investors carried the month.

The fourth point is sector breadth. The best sector gained +8.50% and the worst sector delivered -4.20%, a spread of 12.70 percentage points. That magnitude means portfolio construction mattered more than index direction, and passive VN-Index exposure would not have captured the full divergence under the surface.

## Macro Regime
The macro regime for January 2026 was defined by the interaction between DXY, USD/VND, WTI, gold, and BTC. DXY closed at 96.99 with a -1.31% monthly move. USD/VND closed at 25,950 with a -1.31% monthly move. Those two numbers are the core FX channel, and the domestic currency matters because it shapes SBV liquidity posture, foreign investor confidence, import-cost expectations, and the willingness of local investors to use leverage.

FX was the cleanest tailwind: both DXY and USD/VND declined, giving SBV room to keep financial conditions stable while local investors rebuilt equity exposure. This point should not be treated as a generic macro overlay. For Vietnam, FX stability is part of the equity risk premium. If USD/VND is calm, investors can focus on earnings revisions, sector rotation, and domestic liquidity. If USD/VND becomes unstable, the market starts pricing the risk of policy tightening, dollar hoarding, and weaker foreign participation. That is why a small monthly move in USD/VND can still have a large psychological effect when it happens near a stress zone.

WTI closed at USD 65.21 with a +13.57% move. The oil gain was meaningful enough to benefit selected upstream names but remained below the level that typically pressures CPI or administered-price policy, so the net market effect was contained — energy sector support without a broad cost-shock penalty.

Gold and BTC added a second layer of risk-sentiment information. Gold closed at USD 4,713.90 with a +8.98% monthly move, while BTC closed at USD 84,128.66 with a -3.86% monthly move. The combination — strong gold alongside weak BTC — is a typically defensive cross-asset read, suggesting some hedge demand even as equity investors were buying domestic beta.

## VN-Index Monthly Review
The VN-Index path matters because the same monthly return can come from very different investor behavior. In January 2026, the index opened at 1,733.39, reached a high of 1,918.46, printed a low of 1,731.63, and closed at 1,829.04. The support level for the next month is 1,731.63; the resistance level is 1,918.46. Those are not arbitrary chart points. They are the places where investors either proved willing to defend risk or refused to pay higher prices.

The opening level sets the benchmark for the month. Closing above the open at 1,733.39 confirmed that buyers controlled the final outcome; the monthly high at 1,918.46 marks where marginal demand stopped chasing, and the low at 1,731.63 marks where sellers were met with enough support to hold. Together, these four numbers define the tape far better than the percent change alone.

The +5.52% monthly move should be read as a domestic-liquidity-led rally, with price momentum stronger than ownership quality. The close at 1,829.04 was well above the low but materially below the high, consistent with a market where domestic buyers absorbed offshore supply without triggering a full breakout. That keeps the focus on both price and sponsorship heading into February.

Liquidity completes the review. VND 25,847bn of average daily value across 23 sessions shows that this was an investable tape. There was enough turnover for institutional and active domestic accounts to reposition. That matters because technical levels are more reliable when they are formed on real liquidity. A support level formed on thin holiday trading is weaker. A support level formed after heavy value traded is more meaningful because it reflects actual balance-sheet commitment.

## Foreign Flow and Ownership Quality
Foreign investors bought VND 92,455.90bn and sold VND 98,479.26bn, resulting in net selling of VND 6,023.35bn. The figures are based on HOSE foreign-trading data available for the month, keeping the flow discussion anchored to reported buy and sell values rather than proxy values.

For January 2026, the index rose +5.52% while foreigners net sold VND 6,023.35bn — a rise with selling configuration. That means domestic investors absorbed offshore supply; the rally was real on price and liquidity but lacked offshore endorsement. Technology was the leading sector at +8.50%, and the combination of domestic-driven leadership with foreign net selling confirms that local sponsorship, not offshore accumulation, was the primary force.

Data coverage is sufficient to anchor the analysis: VND 92,455.90bn bought against VND 98,479.26bn sold gives a reliable net figure. The conclusion stands — ownership quality was weaker than the price outcome implied, and further improvement in the setup requires either a foreign-flow turn or a visible broadening of domestic-institutional buying.

## Sector Rotation Map
Technology leadership at +8.50% was consistent with the macro backdrop of a softer DXY and stable VND — conditions that favour quality growth and earnings visibility over cyclical or commodity exposure. Real Estate's -4.20% was equally legible: balance-sheet leverage and refinancing concerns remained hard to underwrite at current rates. The 12.70-percentage-point spread confirms that investors were not buying Vietnam beta indiscriminately — they were selecting earnings duration and macro alignment. That the leader matched the macro backdrop makes the rotation easier to interpret and harder to dismiss as pure positioning noise.

## Global Cross-Asset Synthesis
The global cross-asset picture should be translated into Vietnam-specific channels. DXY closed at 96.99, USD/VND at 25,950, gold at USD 4,713.90, WTI at USD 65.21, and BTC at USD 84,128.66. These are not just dashboard numbers. Each one maps into a different part of the Vietnam equity risk premium.

Read as one synthesis rather than asset by asset, January's cross-asset backdrop was supportive but not unambiguously risk-on: the FX channel was a clean tailwind, the oil move was a manageable headwind, and the gold-strong/BTC-weak pair left commodity risk sentiment hedged. That mix is consistent with a market that could re-risk on domestic liquidity without a full global risk-on confirmation.

## Risk Scenarios and Technical Levels
The technical framework for February starts with support at 1,731.63 and resistance at 1,918.46. A close through either level should be treated as signal, not noise. Support is where buyers previously defended the market. Resistance is where marginal demand previously failed. If the index closes below support, investors should assume the prior range has broken. If the index closes above resistance, investors should assume underweight accounts may need to chase.

The bull case requires three things. First, foreign selling must slow or turn into buying. Second, liquidity must remain high enough to absorb supply without forcing price concessions. Third, leadership must broaden beyond Technology so the index is not dependent on one sector. If those conditions are met, the market can extend toward a higher range and convert the current setup into a more durable trend.

The base case is range trading. In that scenario, the VN-Index holds above 1,731.63 but fails to clear 1,918.46. Sector rotation remains active, domestic liquidity remains the main support, and foreign flow is either mixed or modestly negative. This is a stock-picking regime. It rewards sector selection and punishes passive assumptions. It also requires risk controls because breakouts and breakdowns can fail quickly when ownership quality is not decisive.

The bear case is a support break with renewed foreign outflow. That would imply the market is losing domestic absorption capacity at the same time offshore accounts are reducing risk. The trigger could be FX pressure, a WTI shock, disappointing macro data, weak earnings revisions, or a policy headline. In that case, the first response should be to reduce cyclical beta, avoid crowded winners that are losing momentum, and wait for either foreign-flow stabilization or a reclaim of the broken support.

## The Month Ahead
The main risk was that the rally had moved faster than foreign-flow confirmation, leaving the index vulnerable if leaders stalled. The practical stance for February is therefore conditional rather than dogmatic. The index level matters, but the quality of the move matters more. A breakout with weak breadth and foreign selling is less compelling than a smaller advance with improving ownership and broader sector participation. A pullback with stable foreign flow is less dangerous than a pullback with accelerating offshore supply.

The first data point to watch is foreign net flow. The second is USD/VND. The third is WTI. The fourth is whether Technology continues to lead or whether leadership broadens into banks, securities, industrials, and consumption. Those indicators together will say whether the market is entering a healthier accumulation phase or simply rotating inside a fragile range. They should be tracked weekly, not only at month-end.

For portfolio construction, the right approach is to respect the range and avoid over-interpreting a single headline index close. Above 1,918.46, upside momentum deserves more respect. Below 1,731.63, capital preservation becomes more important than buying dips. Inside the range, sector selection matters most. Investors should prefer sectors with improving earnings visibility, clean ownership support, and macro alignment, while avoiding sectors where price strength is purely a function of positioning squeeze.

The final takeaway is straightforward: monthly notes cannot be short because the market is not one-dimensional. Price, liquidity, foreign ownership, sector breadth, FX, oil, gold, and global risk appetite all interact. A useful Monthly Market View has to connect those pieces, explain where confidence is high, state where data coverage is incomplete, and define the levels that would prove the thesis wrong. That is the standard this note applies to January 2026.

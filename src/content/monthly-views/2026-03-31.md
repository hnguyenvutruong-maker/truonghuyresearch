## Executive Summary
March 2026 should be read as defensive, with the burden of proof shifting decisively to buyers. The VN-Index opened at 1,861.32, traded as high as 1,899.44, fell as low as 1,586.44, and closed at 1,674.49. That produced a -10.04% month, so the headline result was a decline; but the real story is the interaction between the monthly candle, the liquidity base, the foreign-flow line, and the sector map. Average daily liquidity reached VND 26,051bn across 24 sessions, which means the move cannot be dismissed as a thin or purely technical print. There was enough value traded for the market to reveal real positioning preferences.

The month was shaped by a setup that began near prior highs but quickly turned into a macro and ownership stress test. Foreign investors recorded net selling of VND 17,607.83bn, with VND 80,350.98bn of buy value against VND 97,958.81bn of sell value where data is available. That ownership signal is crucial. A market can rise with foreign selling if domestic liquidity is deep, but that kind of rally is more dependent on margin appetite, broker balance sheets, and the willingness of local institutions to absorb supply. A market that falls with foreign selling has a cleaner defensive message because local and offshore investors are aligned in reducing risk.

Sector rotation also mattered. Oil & Gas was the best sector at +12.40%, while Technology was the worst sector at -15.20%, leaving a leader-laggard spread of 27.60 percentage points. That spread says the market was not moving as one block. Investors were making clear choices about which earnings streams, balance sheets, and macro exposures were worth owning. The practical portfolio read is therefore not simply whether the VN-Index was up or down. It is whether the index move had sponsorship, breadth, and macro alignment. For March 2026, the answer is mixed: the tape offered tradable signals, but the quality of those signals depended heavily on foreign flow and sector leadership.

## Market Structure and Tape Quality
The first structural point is the relationship between the open, the close, and the monthly range. The index closed at 1,674.49, well below the opening of 1,861.32, and the high-low range from 1,586.44 to 1,899.44 was the widest of the five-month sample. The close at 1,674.49 sat closer to the low than the high — a distribution candle that signals supply dominated the month's price discovery.

The second point is liquidity. Average daily turnover of VND 26,051bn across 24 sessions — the highest in the five-month sample — makes this decline unambiguously a high-conviction sell. When a market falls sharply on the strongest liquidity in the series, the tape is not a thin panic; it is institutional and active accounts choosing to exit at scale.

The third point is ownership quality. Foreign net selling of VND 17,607.83bn — nearly three times January's pace — aligned with the domestic price decline. This is the clearest defensive alignment in the five-month period: both local and offshore accounts were reducing risk simultaneously, removing the domestic-absorption cushion that limited prior months' drawdowns.

The fourth point is sector breadth. The 27.60-percentage-point spread between Oil & Gas (+12.40%) and Technology (-15.20%) is the widest in the sample. It confirms a bifurcated market where commodity exposure was the only winning factor: every non-energy sector was being re-priced lower.

## Macro Regime
The macro regime for March 2026 was defined by the interaction between DXY, USD/VND, WTI, gold, and BTC. DXY closed at 100.51 with a +2.17% monthly move. USD/VND closed at 26,335 with a +1.13% monthly move. Those two numbers are the core FX channel. A stronger DXY and a weaker VND tightened the EM allocation backdrop: offshore investors faced currency headwinds on both their equity positions and their repatriation economics, compounding the incentive to reduce Vietnam exposure. The domestic currency matters because it shapes SBV liquidity posture, foreign investor confidence, import-cost expectations, and the willingness of local investors to use leverage.

Together with the oil spike discussed below, this FX tightening made March the hardest macro regime in the five-month sample. This point should not be treated as a generic macro overlay. For Vietnam, FX stability is part of the equity risk premium. If USD/VND is calm, investors can focus on earnings revisions, sector rotation, and domestic liquidity. If USD/VND becomes unstable, the market starts pricing the risk of policy tightening, dollar hoarding, and weaker foreign participation. That is why a small monthly move in USD/VND can still have a large psychological effect when it happens near a stress zone.

WTI closed at USD 102.88 with a +44.43% move — by far the largest single monthly oil move in the sample. At USD 102.88, oil was above the threshold that typically pressures CPI and administered-price policy in Vietnam. Transport costs, manufacturing inputs, aviation margins, and retail purchasing power all faced simultaneous headwinds. Oil & Gas names were the one beneficiary (+12.40%), but for every other sector, the oil shock was an earnings and sentiment tax.

Gold and BTC added a second layer of risk-sentiment information. Gold closed at USD 4,526.00 with a -14.51% monthly move — reversing February's surge — while BTC closed near flat at USD 66,691.45 (-0.45%). The gold reversal is notable: after a defensive surge in February, gold sold off sharply in March, which may reflect forced liquidation across risk assets rather than a recovery in investor confidence. Neither gold strength nor BTC strength was available as a stabilizing cross-asset signal this month.

## VN-Index Monthly Review
The VN-Index path matters because the same monthly return can come from very different investor behavior. In March 2026, the index opened at 1,861.32, reached a high of 1,899.44, printed a low of 1,586.44, and closed at 1,674.49. The support level for the next month is 1,586.44; the resistance level is 1,899.44. Those are not arbitrary chart points. They are the places where investors either proved willing to defend risk or refused to pay higher prices.

The opening level sets the benchmark for the month. Closing at 1,674.49 against an open of 1,861.32 means supply dominated decisively — not a marginal miss but a -10.04% month where the high at 1,899.44 was set near the opening before the market turned. The low at 1,586.44 was the floor tested under maximum stress; the close at 1,674.49 sits approximately 28% of the range above the low, confirming a partial but incomplete recovery from the trough. That pattern — wide range, close in the lower quarter, decisive underperformance — is this was a high-volume drawdown in which forced selling, foreign outflow, and oil-linked macro pressure aligned.

Liquidity completes the review. VND 26,051bn of average daily value across 24 sessions shows that this was an investable tape. There was enough turnover for institutional and active domestic accounts to reposition. That matters because technical levels are more reliable when they are formed on real liquidity. A support level formed on thin holiday trading is weaker. A support level formed after heavy value traded is more meaningful because it reflects actual balance-sheet commitment.

## Foreign Flow and Ownership Quality
Foreign investors bought VND 80,350.98bn and sold VND 97,958.81bn, resulting in net selling of VND 17,607.83bn. The figures are based on HOSE foreign-trading data available for the month, keeping the flow discussion anchored to reported buy and sell values rather than proxy values.

For March 2026, net selling of VND 17,607.83bn — VND 80,350.98bn bought against VND 97,958.81bn sold — was the largest single-month foreign outflow in the five-month sample. The index fell -10.04% with this outflow, confirming the clearest defensive alignment in the series: local and offshore accounts reducing risk simultaneously, with no domestic-absorption cushion strong enough to stabilise the market. Oil & Gas was the best sector at +12.40%, which is consistent with foreign portfolio rotation into the one Vietnamese sector that benefited directly from the WTI spike — a selective reallocation within Vietnam, not broad-based buying. Data coverage is sufficient and the flow signal is unambiguous.

## Sector Rotation Map
Oil & Gas at +12.40% was a direct commodity-shock beneficiary, not a sign of broad market health. The sector gained because WTI surged +44.43% — upstream earnings were revised sharply higher — while the rest of the market absorbed the cost-shock consequences. Technology at -15.20% bore the largest single-sector loss: duration re-pricing in a rising-dollar, oil-shock environment is typically fastest and deepest in high-multiple growth names. The 27.60-percentage-point leader-laggard spread is the widest in the sample and confirms that March was not a bearish index month with internal resilience — it was a broad-based drawdown where only commodity exposure held positive.

## Global Cross-Asset Synthesis
The global cross-asset picture should be translated into Vietnam-specific channels. DXY closed at 100.51, USD/VND at 26,335, gold at USD 4,526.00, WTI at USD 102.88, and BTC at USD 66,691.45. These are not just dashboard numbers. Each one maps into a different part of the Vietnam equity risk premium.

Read as one synthesis rather than asset by asset, March's defining feature was that every cross-asset channel transmitted a negative signal at once — a tightening FX channel, an oil shock, and the absence of any safe-haven support as gold reversed its February role. With no cross-asset stabiliser available, that convergence amplified the foreign outflow and the index drawdown.

## Risk Scenarios and Technical Levels
The technical framework for April starts with support at 1,586.44 and resistance at 1,899.44. A close through either level should be treated as signal, not noise. Support is where buyers previously defended the market. Resistance is where marginal demand previously failed. If the index closes below support, investors should assume the prior range has broken. If the index closes above resistance, investors should assume underweight accounts may need to chase.

The bull case requires three things. First, foreign selling must slow or turn into buying. Second, liquidity must remain high enough to absorb supply without forcing price concessions. Third, leadership must broaden beyond Oil & Gas so the index is not dependent on one sector. If those conditions are met, the market can extend toward a higher range and convert the current setup into a more durable trend.

The base case is range trading. In that scenario, the VN-Index holds above 1,586.44 but fails to clear 1,899.44. Sector rotation remains active, domestic liquidity remains the main support, and foreign flow is either mixed or modestly negative. This is a stock-picking regime. It rewards sector selection and punishes passive assumptions. It also requires risk controls because breakouts and breakdowns can fail quickly when ownership quality is not decisive.

The bear case is a support break with renewed foreign outflow. That would imply the market is losing domestic absorption capacity at the same time offshore accounts are reducing risk. The trigger could be FX pressure, a WTI shock, disappointing macro data, weak earnings revisions, or a policy headline. In that case, the first response should be to reduce cyclical beta, avoid crowded winners that are losing momentum, and wait for either foreign-flow stabilization or a reclaim of the broken support.

## The Month Ahead
The main risk was that rebounds from the 1,586 low would be tactical unless foreign selling slowed and Technology found a durable floor. The practical stance for April is therefore conditional rather than dogmatic. The index level matters, but the quality of the move matters more. A breakout with weak breadth and foreign selling is less compelling than a smaller advance with improving ownership and broader sector participation. A pullback with stable foreign flow is less dangerous than a pullback with accelerating offshore supply.

The first data point to watch is foreign net flow. The second is USD/VND. The third is WTI. The fourth is whether Oil & Gas continues to lead or whether leadership broadens into banks, securities, industrials, and consumption. Those indicators together will say whether the market is entering a healthier accumulation phase or simply rotating inside a fragile range. They should be tracked weekly, not only at month-end.

For portfolio construction, the right approach is to respect the range and avoid over-interpreting a single headline index close. Above 1,899.44, upside momentum deserves more respect. Below 1,586.44, capital preservation becomes more important than buying dips. Inside the range, sector selection matters most. Investors should prefer sectors with improving earnings visibility, clean ownership support, and macro alignment, while avoiding sectors where price strength is purely a function of positioning squeeze.

The final takeaway is straightforward: monthly notes cannot be short because the market is not one-dimensional. Price, liquidity, foreign ownership, sector breadth, FX, oil, gold, and global risk appetite all interact. A useful Monthly Market View has to connect those pieces, explain where confidence is high, state where data coverage is incomplete, and define the levels that would prove the thesis wrong. That is the standard this note applies to March 2026.

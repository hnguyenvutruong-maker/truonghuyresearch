## Executive Summary
February 2026 should be read as neutral, with stronger index resilience than underlying ownership quality. The VN-Index opened at 1,890.39, traded as high as 1,899.44, fell as low as 1,741.37, and closed at 1,880.33. That produced a -0.53% month, so the headline result was a decline; but the real story is the interaction between the monthly candle, the liquidity base, the foreign-flow line, and the sector map. Average daily liquidity reached VND 22,575bn across 21 sessions, which means the move cannot be dismissed as a thin or purely technical print. There was enough value traded for the market to reveal real positioning preferences.

The month was shaped by digestion of January's rally, Tet-related liquidity distortions, and a rotation away from the prior growth leaders. Foreign investors recorded net selling of VND 6,935.42bn, with VND 22,536.71bn of buy value against VND 29,472.12bn of sell value where data is available. That ownership signal is crucial. A market can rise with foreign selling if domestic liquidity is deep, but that kind of rally is more dependent on margin appetite, broker balance sheets, and the willingness of local institutions to absorb supply. A market that falls with foreign selling has a cleaner defensive message because local and offshore investors are aligned in reducing risk.

Sector rotation also mattered. Banking was the best sector at +5.30%, while Technology was the worst sector at -7.80%, leaving a leader-laggard spread of 13.10 percentage points. That spread says the market was not moving as one block. Investors were making clear choices about which earnings streams, balance sheets, and macro exposures were worth owning. The practical portfolio read is therefore not simply whether the VN-Index was up or down. It is whether the index move had sponsorship, breadth, and macro alignment. For February 2026, the answer is mixed: the tape offered tradable signals, but the quality of those signals depended heavily on foreign flow and sector leadership.

## Market Structure and Tape Quality
The first structural point is the relationship between the open, the close, and the monthly range. The index closed at 1,880.33, below the opening of 1,890.39 — supply won the month marginally. The high-low range from 1,741.37 to 1,899.44 was wide relative to the flat close, indicating a market that absorbed a sharp mid-month drawdown and recovered, but could not sustain the January high zone.

The second point is liquidity. Average daily turnover of VND 22,575bn across 21 sessions, down from January's VND 25,847bn, still validated the monthly candle as a genuine market signal. The lower volume relative to January, however, is consistent with Tet-related distortions compressing active days.

The third point is ownership quality. Foreign investors were net sellers of VND 6,935.42bn, slightly above January's VND 6,023.35bn, extending the streak of domestic-carried tape. The fact that the index nearly held flat despite this continued offshore supply is the key resilience observation.

The fourth point is sector breadth. The 13.10-percentage-point spread between Banking (+5.30%) and Technology (-7.80%) was wider than January's 12.70-point spread, confirming that under-the-surface rotation was the dominant story, not directional index movement.

## Macro Regime
The macro regime for February 2026 was defined by the interaction between DXY, USD/VND, WTI, gold, and BTC. DXY closed at 97.61 with a flat +0.00% monthly move. USD/VND closed at 26,030 with a +0.58% monthly move. The divergence — USD/VND edging higher while DXY was unchanged — signalled a mild local dollar-demand premium, adding a small headwind to foreign-investor confidence even without a global dollar move. The domestic currency matters because it shapes SBV liquidity posture, foreign investor confidence, import-cost expectations, and the willingness of local investors to use leverage.

This FX read should not be treated as a generic macro overlay. For Vietnam, FX stability is part of the equity risk premium. If USD/VND is calm, investors can focus on earnings revisions, sector rotation, and domestic liquidity. If USD/VND becomes unstable, the market starts pricing the risk of policy tightening, dollar hoarding, and weaker foreign participation. That is why a small monthly move in USD/VND can still have a large psychological effect when it happens near a stress zone.

WTI closed at USD 67.02 with a +7.85% move. The oil gain was modest in absolute terms and did not reach a threshold that would typically trigger administered-price pressure; it was therefore a mild cost headwind for retail, aviation, and logistics but not a CPI shock that would force SBV to tighten.

Gold and BTC added a second layer of risk-sentiment information. Gold closed at USD 5,230.50 with a +13.15% monthly move — the strongest single-asset return in the cross-asset set — while BTC collapsed -16.20% to USD 65,881.80. The gold surge alongside a BTC crash is unambiguously defensive: investors were seeking stores of value and hedging uncertainty rather than adding speculative risk. That cross-asset signal sat at odds with the equity market's relatively stable close.

## VN-Index Monthly Review
The VN-Index path matters because the same monthly return can come from very different investor behavior. In February 2026, the index opened at 1,890.39, reached a high of 1,899.44, printed a low of 1,741.37, and closed at 1,880.33. The support level for the next month is 1,741.37; the resistance level is 1,899.44. Those are not arbitrary chart points. They are the places where investors either proved willing to defend risk or refused to pay higher prices.

The opening level sets the benchmark for the month. Closing at 1,880.33 against an open of 1,890.39 means supply won the month marginally; the high at 1,899.44 showed buyers tested January's ceiling but could not clear it, while the low at 1,741.37 showed sellers tested deeply before being absorbed. The close sitting near the upper third of the range is the resilience signal: the market took a large intra-month hit and recovered most of it.

The -0.53% monthly move should be read as a consolidation tape where Banking carried the index while Technology corrected. The structure — wide range, close near the upper end, marginally below the open — is consistent with a market that absorbed forced selling without breaking its longer-term trend.

Liquidity completes the review. VND 22,575bn of average daily value across 21 sessions shows that this was an investable tape. There was enough turnover for institutional and active domestic accounts to reposition. That matters because technical levels are more reliable when they are formed on real liquidity. A support level formed on thin holiday trading is weaker. A support level formed after heavy value traded is more meaningful because it reflects actual balance-sheet commitment.

## Foreign Flow and Ownership Quality
Foreign investors bought VND 22,536.71bn and sold VND 29,472.12bn, resulting in net selling of VND 6,935.42bn. The figures are based on HOSE foreign-trading data available for the month, keeping the flow discussion anchored to reported buy and sell values rather than proxy values.

For February 2026, net selling of VND 6,935.42bn — VND 22,536.71bn bought against VND 29,472.12bn sold — extended January's offshore-exit pattern. The index fell only -0.53% despite this continued pressure, which means domestic investors again absorbed offshore supply and prevented a deeper decline. Banking's leadership at +5.30% is consistent with domestic institutional preference for earnings-visible, dividend-paying names rather than growth recovery plays. The fact that Technology reversed to worst sector at -7.80% after January's leadership is a key signal: the January growth rally unwound quickly when offshore accounts reduced, suggesting the prior month's strength had a significant foreign component that did not persist.

## Sector Rotation Map
Banking at +5.30% was the clear defensive anchor: in a month where gold surged +13.15%, BTC collapsed -16.20%, and foreign investors net sold, domestic accounts rotated toward balance-sheet quality and earnings stability. Technology's -7.80% reversal — from January's +8.50% best sector to February's worst — is the most informative data point in the rotation map. It says January's growth leadership was positionally fragile: it partially depended on foreign participation that did not carry over after the Tet break. The 13.10-percentage-point spread confirms that sector selection, not index direction, was the primary return driver this month.

## Global Cross-Asset Synthesis
The global cross-asset picture should be translated into Vietnam-specific channels. DXY closed at 97.61, USD/VND at 26,030, gold at USD 5,230.50, WTI at USD 67.02, and BTC at USD 65,881.80. These are not just dashboard numbers. Each one maps into a different part of the Vietnam equity risk premium.

Read as one synthesis rather than asset by asset, February's defining feature was the gap between a near-flat Vietnam equity close and a clearly deteriorating global risk backdrop — a firming local dollar premium, a contained oil move, and the sharpest gold-up/BTC-down divergence in the five-month sample. The cross-asset backdrop therefore amplified the defensive read from the foreign-flow data rather than offsetting it.

## Risk Scenarios and Technical Levels
The technical framework for March starts with support at 1,741.37 and resistance at 1,899.44. A close through either level should be treated as signal, not noise. Support is where buyers previously defended the market. Resistance is where marginal demand previously failed. If the index closes below support, investors should assume the prior range has broken. If the index closes above resistance, investors should assume underweight accounts may need to chase.

The bull case requires three things. First, foreign selling must slow or turn into buying. Second, liquidity must remain high enough to absorb supply without forcing price concessions. Third, leadership must broaden beyond Banking so the index is not dependent on one sector. If those conditions are met, the market can extend toward a higher range and convert the current setup into a more durable trend.

The base case is range trading. In that scenario, the VN-Index holds above 1,741.37 but fails to clear 1,899.44. Sector rotation remains active, domestic liquidity remains the main support, and foreign flow is either mixed or modestly negative. This is a stock-picking regime. It rewards sector selection and punishes passive assumptions. It also requires risk controls because breakouts and breakdowns can fail quickly when ownership quality is not decisive.

The bear case is a support break with renewed foreign outflow. That would imply the market is losing domestic absorption capacity at the same time offshore accounts are reducing risk. The trigger could be FX pressure, a WTI shock, disappointing macro data, weak earnings revisions, or a policy headline. In that case, the first response should be to reduce cyclical beta, avoid crowded winners that are losing momentum, and wait for either foreign-flow stabilization or a reclaim of the broken support.

## The Month Ahead
The main risk was a failed attempt to reclaim 1,900, especially if foreign selling persisted after the holiday period. The practical stance for March is therefore conditional rather than dogmatic. The index level matters, but the quality of the move matters more. A breakout with weak breadth and foreign selling is less compelling than a smaller advance with improving ownership and broader sector participation. A pullback with stable foreign flow is less dangerous than a pullback with accelerating offshore supply.

The first data point to watch is foreign net flow. The second is USD/VND. The third is WTI. The fourth is whether Banking continues to lead or whether leadership broadens into securities, industrials, and consumption. Those indicators together will say whether the market is entering a healthier accumulation phase or simply rotating inside a fragile range. They should be tracked weekly, not only at month-end.

For portfolio construction, the right approach is to respect the range and avoid over-interpreting a single headline index close. Above 1,899.44, upside momentum deserves more respect. Below 1,741.37, capital preservation becomes more important than buying dips. Inside the range, sector selection matters most. Investors should prefer sectors with improving earnings visibility, clean ownership support, and macro alignment, while avoiding sectors where price strength is purely a function of positioning squeeze.

The final takeaway is straightforward: monthly notes cannot be short because the market is not one-dimensional. Price, liquidity, foreign ownership, sector breadth, FX, oil, gold, and global risk appetite all interact. A useful Monthly Market View has to connect those pieces, explain where confidence is high, state where data coverage is incomplete, and define the levels that would prove the thesis wrong. That is the standard this note applies to February 2026.
